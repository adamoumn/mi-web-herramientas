Can you tell if a text was written by AI?

There are clues, but no proof. Here is what you can and cannot claim.

Signals that often show up

  • Generic, neutral tone with no opinions or personal experience.
  • Sentences and paragraphs with a very symmetrical structure.
  • Repeated connectors and very solemn expressions.
  • Few concrete details: real examples, names, places.
  • Data or sources that cannot be verified.

Why they are not proof

All of those signals also appear in human text. A very formal text, written carefully or by someone who is not fluent in the language, can look like AI. Conversely, an AI text that someone has edited heavily can go unnoticed. Detectors give false positives and false negatives, and studies have found bias against people writing in a second language.

How to use a percentage sensibly

  • Treat it as an indicative clue, not a verdict.
  • Look at the flagged sentences, not only the overall number.
  • If you need to verify authorship, ask for drafts or version history, or talk to the person about the content.
  • Never accuse anyone based only on a tool’s result.

How our detector works

It analyzes each sentence, calculates an estimated percentage and underlines those that look most AI-written. It is an AI-made estimate and can be wrong. If it flags sentences, you can also rewrite them to improve the text.
