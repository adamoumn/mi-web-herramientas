How to summarize a long text without losing what matters

A good summary is not a trimmed text: it is the essentials, said clearly.

Before you start: what do you need it for?

You do not summarize the same way to study as to brief your boss. Decide what you need: the main ideas, the figures, the conclusions or the decisions to make. That determines what goes in.

A six-step method

  1. Skim first. Read the title, subheadings, introduction and conclusion. They give you the skeleton of the text.
  2. Read with a goal. Underline only the main ideas and key data, not whole paragraphs.
  3. Write each idea in one line. In your own words, not copied.
  4. Order them. Group related ideas and remove repeats.
  5. Write it up. Link the ideas clearly.
  6. Compare with the original. Check there are no errors in figures, names or conclusions and nothing important is missing.

How long should it be?

It depends on the use. To decide whether a document is worth reading, one or two sentences are enough. To study or share, a paragraph or a list of key points usually works. The more technical the text, the more you should keep the figures.
